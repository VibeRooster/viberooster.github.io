New vocabulary

What's vibe thinking?

Vibe coding is what happens when you and an agent build something that runs. Vibe thinking is what happens when you and an agent build something that makes sense — a concept, a map of a topic, a model of a decision — and you want the next person to be able to walk it.

Where vibe coding stops

Vibe coding got a name because the production loop had become cheap: you describe, the model writes, you steer by feel. The output is software. The remaining problem is the last mile — getting it onto a URL someone else can open. That's the original Vibe Rooster pitch, and it still holds.

Look at what people actually do with agents, though. Most sessions don't produce an app. They produce orientation: a pricing thesis for Thursday's board. A competitive landscape for one pitch. A causal story about why churn spiked. A half-formed idea that got sharp over forty messages. The work is the thinking. There is no binary to ship.

We didn't have a name for that loop, because its output had nowhere to live except the chat window it happened in.

The capture problem

A good thinking session currently dies where it was born. What you have at the end is a time-series of the thinking — discarded frames, a diagram that only you can still see, the one fork that actually mattered. A transcript preserves the path. It does not preserve the topology. The next person has to walk the whole path to rebuild the map you already have in your head. Almost nobody will.

A summary is the other default: a memo, a paste into a doc, a "here's what we figured out." That keeps the conclusions and throws away the structure — the forks, the alternatives, the one node that actually matters if you drill.

Neither is how you'd want to hand someone a rich idea. The conversation was a lab. What it produced is a model. Those are different artifacts.

The two loops

Vibe codingVibe thinking

Native publication A live URL An explorable at a live URL
Where it dies localhost The chat window

Same cheap loop, different payload. One produces software. The other produces a structure of understanding. Both want the same publishing primitive: a URL that exists for as long as the conversation it serves.

What to keep, what to throw away

FormWhat it keepsWhat it loses
Transcript The path of the thinking The topology — the reader has to walk the whole path
Memo The conclusions The forks, the alternatives, the live nodes
Explorable The structure that survived the session The dead ends — and that's the point

You don't share the conversation. You share the structure that survived it.

The native form is an explorable

An explorable is an interactive artifact the reader navigates instead of reads. For vibe thinking, that's not a flourish. It's the only form that holds what the session actually produced: a structure too rich for a paragraph and too branched for a slide.

The reader expands a node, filters the set, takes the fork you took — or a different one. They control the projection. That's the whole reason to have thought it through with an agent instead of writing a memo: the model that emerged is bigger than one frame, and the next person shouldn't have to sit through your forty messages to get at it.

How to tell a real capture from a chat dump

✓ The real thing

You extract the model — nodes, relationships, the live forks — and give the next person control of the projection. Someone who wasn't in the session can navigate what you figured out, at the depth they need.

✗ Theater

You pretty-print the transcript, add a table of contents, call it a research report. That's the conversation wearing a costume. It looks published and travels no better than the chat it came from.

The rule, stated flatly: the upgrade is not "the chat looks nicer." The upgrade is "someone who wasn't in the session can walk the structure."

Why this is a publishing problem

Until recently, turning a thinking session into something shareable meant a designer, a deck, or an afternoon in a diagramming tool. So most vibe thinking never left the chat. AI collapsed the production cost of a custom visual the same way it collapsed the production cost of an app. The thinking can now hatch as cheaply as the software.

Same loop, different payload: vibe it, hatch it, roost it. Live for the meeting. Expire after. Keep it if the idea turns out to be a keeper.

One honest caveat. Cheap thinking is still thinking. A wrong model in a polished explorable looks like knowledge — data-viz styling lends authority the session may not have earned. Trace it back to ground truth before you share it. Provenance is a problem we take seriously.

If the interesting thing happened in a chat, don't ship the chat. Hatch the map.
