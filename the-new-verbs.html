The words

The new verbs.

People say Xerox when they mean the copy, and Kleenex when they mean the tissue, because the brand word got there first and stayed shorter than the description. Hatch, roost, pin, and pack are that kind of word. The barnyard is the language. Translate it back into tenant, site, and workspace and you are talking about a different product.

Four words

Hatch is one live site at https://{slug}.{apex}. recipe-finder.theroost.dev is a hatch. So is a listing on theroost.homes. A free hatch stays up for 48 hours and holds 100 MB. The verb is hatch: publish that site. The connector that does the publishing is also called Hatch — the MCP at https://mcp.theroost.dev/mcp. When you are naming the thing that went live, you mean the hatch at the apex.

Roost is the container an agent works inside — what the product used to call a workspace. There are two plans. Roost is $259 a month: fair-use hatches that still expire, 10 subscription Pins, durable HITL, unlimited members. Roost Audit is $459 a month: that same roost, plus an exportable audit trail and a year of HITL retention. Org stays org. It is the billing account, and it is not one of these four words.

Pin means the hatch does not expire. On the price list that is a year: $4.99 keeps one hatch, with 1 GB included. A roost already includes 10 Pins while the plan is active. The verb is pin.

Pack is the add-on capacity. $4.99 a year per gigabyte, one gigabyte per Pack, on a hatch or a Pin. Stack them if you need more. Pack is space. Pin is time.

What to say

Kleenex was never a verb, and roost is mostly a noun. The test is the same either way: you stop reaching for the longer, older label.

Tenant means hatch. Site, when you mean the thing at the URL, means hatch. Workspace means roost. “Upgrade to forever” means pin it.

The homepage still labels its third step Roost it: share the link, then decide what happens to it. The noun has settled underneath that step. The URL is the hatch. The container is the roost.
